A full-fledged Transport department functions in the college with a fleet of 19 buses providing transport facility to students and staff from various places across the Vellore region. The number of buses is increased corresponding to the increase in the intake of students, ensuring comfortable commutes for the entire campus community.

Each bus is assigned an experienced driver with a dedicated route, and the fleet is maintained under the supervision of the Transport In-charge and a technician team. The buses operate on a fixed schedule for both morning (arrival) and afternoon (departure) sessions, with well-defined pickup and drop points to maximize coverage while minimizing travel time for students and staff.

Mess

The Mess facility at Kingston Engineering College is dedicated to providing wholesome home-style meals to hostel residents and day scholars. Operating multiple sessions throughout the day for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, the mess ensures that students receive balanced and nutritious food at highly subsidized and affordable rates, without compromising on quality.

Our mess operates under the supervision of qualified catering staff who prioritize cleanliness, taste, and nutrition in every meal served. Regular feedback is collected from mess committee members and students to improve the quality, variety, and overall dining experience throughout the academic year.

Water Conservation

Rain Water Harvesting: The institution has implemented a rainwater harvesting system throughout the campus to ensure proper collection of rainwater. The rainwater gathering pits and tanks are built according to recommended industry standards. The rainwater is collected through the shallow ditches placed strategically which run throughout the length of the campus. The terraces and the roofs of the buildings in the campus are fitted with pipes that are routed to the rainwater collecting pipes. All the collected rainwater is recycled to be used and to replenish the groundwater as well.

Borewell / Open Well Recharge: Bore well / open well recharge is an effective method used to replenish groundwater levels by allowing rainwater or surface water to percolate into underground aquifers. The installation of this system by our institute is a step forward in improving our water conservation ability. The benefits of this method are water level rises and 5 bore wells are recharged so there is no problem of water clogging or low water level.

Construction of Tanks and Bunds: The Tanks and Bunds are designed to store liquids in large quantities. The seven tanks are fixed at various places in the campus as a part of our water conservation facilities. There is also one more main sump in our college campus with a capacity of 2,99,694 litres.

Maintenance of Water Bodies and Distribution: The groundwater is pumped into the storage tank located at different locations on the campus there are a few overhead tanks on the campus the water is distributed through a well-laid pipe network. Driving water is supplied through a separate set of distribution pipes and water for other purposes has its own set of pipe networks. The maintenance of the pipe distribution network is done by a dedicated staff team assigned for this particular task, the institution has also spread awareness of the best water to all its staff and students.

RO Water Plant in the Campus: The RO water plant is available at different locations with a capacity of 6750 liters per day in our campus. The purifier water is distributed through a well-laid pipe network. The RO water is supplied through separate pipes for drinking and cooking purposes have its own set of pipe networks. The maintenance of the pipe distribution network is done by a dedicated staff team assigned for this particular task, the institution has also spread awareness of best water to all its staff and students.
